\begin{abstract}
This restriction-version report is separate from \code{nkpc\_hsa\_report.pdf},
which remains the historical reduced-form and ablation report. The present
document asks one question: does the local Rotemberg--HSA cross-equation
restriction survive contact with the data? It compares a fixed-reference local
benchmark (F0) with an unrestricted moving-reference model (U), the
cross-restricted model (R1), its weak Third-law extension (R2), and a
constant-pass-through benchmark (R3), on \TheoryObservationCount{} quarters from
\TheorySampleStart{} to \TheorySampleEnd. Every numerical table, figure and
in-text number is generated from the signed theory-run namespace; historical
artifacts are never substituted for a missing current run.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why we estimate this equation: the local Rotemberg--HSA Phillips curve}

\subsection{The HSA Phillips curve}
The mechanism under test is that the slope of the Phillips curve depends on how
many firms compete. In a Rotemberg economy with a CES demand structure, the
slope of the inflation equation with respect to real marginal cost is governed
by the demand elasticity $\zeta$ and the adjustment-cost parameter $\chi$,
through $\kappa=(\zeta-1)/\chi$. The HSA extension makes $\zeta$ itself a
function of the number of competitors $N$: entry raises the elasticity each
firm faces, so a market with more firms passes cost shocks into prices faster.
Falling firm counts then flatten the Phillips curve, which is the empirical
regularity this project was built to assess.

Three statements are logically distinct, and this report keeps them apart
throughout. The \emph{Second law} says that competition raises pass-through:
$\theta_0>0$ and $\kappa_N>0$. The \emph{weak Third law} says that the entry
channel itself weakens as the reference level of competition rises:
$\gamma\le0$. The \emph{cross-equation restriction} says something stronger
than either --- that the same structural object governs both channels, so the
slope response and the entry coefficient are not free to move independently.

\subsection{Two ways competition enters}
The estimating equation lets competition act through two separate objects, and
the distinction is what makes a cross-equation restriction meaningful at all.

The \emph{reference level} $\Nbar_t$ is a slow-moving local anchor: the level of
competition around which the firm's problem is linearised. It shifts the
coefficients themselves, $\kt=\kappa_0+\kappa_N\Nbar_t$ and
$\theta_t=\theta_0+\gamma\Nbar_t$. The \emph{cycle} $\Nhat_t$ is the deviation
of observed competition from that anchor, and it enters the inflation equation
directly as a regressor with coefficient $\theta_t$.

F0 shuts the first channel off. It fixes the reference at a single $N_0$, so
$\kt=\kappa_0$ for every period and only the cycle matters. Everything the
restricted models say about $\kappa_N$ is therefore invisible in F0, which is
why F0 is a benchmark and not a nested special case.

\subsection{From theory to the estimating equation}
With $\Nbar$ measured in ten log points from the anchor
($\Nbar=10(\log N-\log N_0)$, anchor $N_0=\TheoryNZeroAnchor$ in the raw units
of the competition series) and inflation in percentage points, the quarterly
structural identity $d\kappa/d\log N=\zeta\Theta$ maps into the stored
empirical coefficients as
\[
  \frac{d\kappa}{d\log N}=10\,\kappa_N,
  \qquad
  100\,\kappa_N=b_x\,\zeta_0\,\theta_0 .
\]
The second equality is the cross-equation restriction. It is imposed with
$\zeta_0=\TheoryZetaZero$ held fixed (implying a flexible-price markup
$\mu_0=\TheoryMuZero$) and an explicitly configured marginal-cost loading
$b_x=\TheoryMarginalCostLoading$. The loading is never defaulted to one: a
regression on an activity \emph{gap} would need its own $b_x$, and the code
refuses to impose the identity without one.

\subsection{Distance from the theory}
The moving reference is where this report departs from Eq.~21 of the underlying
theory. The theory is a fixed-reference local approximation; letting $\Nbar_t$
drift is a semi-structural device that keeps the local approximation near the
data as competition trends over three decades. The taxonomy below states, for
each object in the model, whether it is a theory identity, a maintained law, a
domain condition, an empirical approximation, or something deliberately left
free.

\par\medskip
\input{../results/tables/theory/restriction_taxonomy.tex}

\par\medskip\noindent
Two identities of the fixed-reference theory --- $\kappa_0=(\zeta_0-1)/\chi$
and $\theta_0=(10/\chi)(1-\rho_0)/\rho_0$ --- are deliberately \emph{not}
imposed. Imposing them would pin $\chi$ from the level of the slope, and the
level of the slope is exactly the quantity the moving-reference approximation is
least entitled to. A model that imposes them is future work (R4), not a
variant estimated here.

\section{Econometric model and estimation}

\subsection{Model variants}
Five models are estimated. Within the moving-reference family the nesting is
$U\supset R1\supset R2$, and R3 is $R1+\{\gamma=0\}$. F0 is not nested in that
family, because its reference is fixed rather than restricted.

\par\medskip
\input{../results/tables/theory/model_hierarchy.tex}

\subsection{The inflation equation actually estimated}
Write $y_t=\pi_t-E_t\pi_{t+1}$ and $a_t=\pi_{t-1}-E_t\pi_{t+1}$. The equation
carried to the data is
\[
  y_t=\alpha\,a_t
      +\underbrace{(\kappa_0+\kappa_N\Nbar_t)}_{\kt}\,x_t
      -\underbrace{(\theta_0+\gamma\Nbar_t)}_{\theta_t}\,\Nhat_t
      +\lambda\,\zeta_t+\eta_t,
  \qquad \zeta_t=x_t-\phi_1x_{t-1},
\]
with $\eta_t\sim N(0,\sigma_\eta^2)$. The term $\lambda\zeta_t$ carries the
correlation between the inflation disturbance and the activity innovation, so
$\sigma_\eta^2$ is the conditional variance and $\sigma_e^2=\lambda^2
\sigma_\zeta^2+\sigma_\eta^2$ the total.

Under the cross-equation restriction the two competition regressors collapse
into one. Substituting $\kappa_N=b_x\zeta_0\theta_0/100$,
\[
  \kappa_N\,x_t\Nbar_t-\theta_0\Nhat_t
  =\theta_0\Big[\tfrac{b_x\zeta_0}{100}\,x_t\Nbar_t-\Nhat_t\Big],
\]
so R1--R3 sample one fewer coefficient than U and $\kappa_N$ is \emph{derived}
rather than drawn. This is the sense in which the restriction is exact rather
than a shrinkage prior: it removes a dimension from the coefficient block.

\subsection{State-space representation}
F0 and the moving-reference family use genuinely different state systems.

F0 carries a single competition deviation $q_t$ around the fixed anchor, with an
AR(2) transition and a measurement equation that is active only in the quarters
where the annual series is observed. Its state vector is $(q_t,q_{t-1})$; there
is no $\Nbar$ state to move.

U, R1, R2 and R3 carry both a random-walk-with-drift reference $\Nbar_t$ and an
AR(2) cycle $\Nhat_t$, observed jointly through
$N^{obs}_t=\Nhat_t+\Nbar_t+\nu_t$ with $\nu_t\sim N(0,\sigma_N^2)$. Because the
reference multiplies the coefficients, the observation equation is bilinear in
the states whenever $\kappa_N\neq0$ or $\gamma\neq0$ --- which is why the state
update is not a plain FFBS for those models.

\subsection{Priors}
All five models are estimated under one prior specification,
\code{\TheoryPriorSpec}. Coefficient priors are stated in physical units and
converted internally; variance priors for the $N$ block are in squared
ten-log-point units, matched to the scale of the transformed competition series.

\par\medskip
\input{../results/tables/theory/baseline_priors.tex}

\par\medskip\noindent
One asymmetry deserves to be stated before any table is read. In U, $\kappa_N$
carries its own prior. In R1--R3 it has none, because it is a deterministic
function of $\theta_0$; its prior is whatever the $\theta_0$ prior induces
through $\kappa_N=b_x\zeta_0\theta_0/100$. A difference between U's and R1's
posterior for $\kappa_N$ is therefore partly a difference in priors, and
Appendix~\ref{app:priorpost} shows exactly how much.

\subsection{The coefficient block: a truncated-Gaussian coordinate Gibbs}
The maintained laws and the domain conditions are hard constraints, not soft
penalties: $\kappa_0>0$, $\theta_0>0$, $\gamma\le0$ in R2, and --- when path
positivity is required --- $\kt>0$ and $\theta_t>0$ in \emph{every} period. All
of these are linear in the coefficient vector, so the conditional posterior is a
Gaussian truncated to a polyhedron $A\beta\ge\ell$.

Because $\kt$ and $\theta_t$ are affine in $\Nbar_t$, requiring positivity at
the smallest and largest observed $\Nbar$ is equivalent to requiring it at every
date, and the constraint matrix needs only those two rows per path. The block is
then updated with a coordinate Gibbs sweep that is invariant for the exact
truncated conditional. Independent rejection sampling was abandoned here for a
reason visible in the diagnostics: the probe column of
Table~\ref{tab:admissibility} shows that a majority of unconstrained draws would
fall outside the admissible region, so a rejection sampler would stall.

\subsection{The state update: particle Gibbs and the $\gamma=0$ joint FFBS}
For U, R1 and R2 the bilinear observation equation rules out an exact FFBS, and
the latent paths are updated by a conditional bootstrap particle Gibbs sweep.
For R3, $\gamma=0$ makes the joint state linear-Gaussian again, so R3 uses an
exact joint FFBS instead; F0 uses an exact FFBS on its own two-state system.

Whole-path positivity is enforced on the state draw by proposing from the
unconstrained conditional and keeping the previous path when the proposal
violates it. Since the proposal is the exact conditional, this is an
independence Metropolis step whose acceptance ratio is one inside the admissible
set --- the target is the correctly truncated conditional, not an approximation
of it. The rejection share is reported, because a high one is evidence that the
local approximation is being asked to span too wide an $N$ range.

\subsection{Iterations, saved draws, and convergence criteria}
The moving-reference models mix far more slowly than F0, and the production
lengths reflect measured pilot behaviour rather than a round number: a 12{,}000
iteration pilot left latent-state ESS in the tens, and a 120{,}000 iteration
follow-up still left $\rho_2$ short, so U, R1 and R2 run at 400{,}000 iterations
on four chains. A run enters this report only if its diagnostics pass; the
builder refuses runs that are quick, dirty-code, stale, or unconverged.

\section{Main results}
Table~\ref{tab:posterior} collects the restriction-relevant coefficients. Three
things stand out, and the rest of the report is an attempt to take each one
seriously.

First, the slope level is estimated tightly and consistently: $\kappa_0$ is
\TheoryFZeroKappaZero{} in F0, \TheoryUKappaZero{} in U, and essentially
unchanged across R1--R3. Nothing in the restriction hierarchy disturbs it.

Second, the unrestricted slope response is near zero and two-sided:
$\kappa_N=\TheoryUKappaN$ in U with a 95\% interval of
$[\TheoryUKappaNLo,\TheoryUKappaNHi]$. Under the restriction it is positive and
bounded away from zero, $\kappa_N=\TheoryROneKappaN$ with interval
$[\TheoryROneKappaNLo,\TheoryROneKappaNHi]$ in R1, because the restriction ties
it to $\theta_0$, which the entry channel does identify.

Third, $\gamma$ is barely informed by the data. In U it is
$\TheoryUGamma$ with interval $[\TheoryUGammaLo,\TheoryUGammaHi]$, and
Appendix~\ref{app:priorpost} shows its posterior sitting almost on top of its
prior. The weak Third law in R2 therefore does most of its work through the
constraint itself, moving $\gamma$ to $\TheoryRTwoGamma$ with interval
$[\TheoryRTwoGammaLo,\TheoryRTwoGammaHi]$.

\section{The cross-equation restriction against the unrestricted posterior}
This is the section the report exists for. U estimates $\kappa_N$ and $\theta_0$
separately, so inside U one can ask directly how far the posterior sits from the
line the restriction would impose.

At U's own $\theta_0$, the restriction would require
$\kappa_N=\TheoryURestrictionValue$. U's posterior mean is
$\TheoryUKappaN$. The gap has posterior mean $\TheoryUGapMean$ with a 90\%
interval of $[\TheoryUGapLo,\TheoryUGapHi]$ and
$P(\text{gap}>0)=\TheoryUProbGapPositive$. The interval contains zero
comfortably, so \emph{the data do not reject the restriction}. But the point
estimate is an order of magnitude below the restricted value, so neither do the
data support it: the unrestricted posterior is simply not informative enough
about $\kappa_N$ to discriminate.

The same conclusion appears more sharply if the identity is inverted. Solving
$100\kappa_N=b_x\zeta_0\theta_0$ for the elasticity gives an implied $\zeta_0$
with posterior median $\TheoryUImpliedZetaMedian$ and
$P(\zeta_0>1)=\TheoryUProbZetaAboveOne$ --- barely better than a coin flip that
the implied elasticity is even in the region where a markup exists. The honest
reading is that $\zeta_0$ is not identified by this data cell, which is why the
production design fixes it at $\TheoryZetaZero$ rather than sampling it.

\section{Does the restriction improve fit?}
The restriction removes a coefficient. Table~\ref{tab:fit} asks what that costs
in the fit of the inflation equation.

\par\medskip
\input{../results/tables/theory/conditional_fit.tex}
\captionof{table}{In-sample fit of the inflation equation, evaluated at the
stored latent paths.}\label{tab:fit}

\par\medskip\noindent
\textbf{What this table is, and what it is not.} The residuals are computed at
each stored draw from the same arithmetic the sampler's own regression uses, so
the numbers are exact for the equation as estimated. But they are
\emph{conditional} on the latent competition path: a model with a richer state
block is not charged for that richness, and the effective-parameter count $p_D$
counts coefficient flexibility only. This is a diagnostic of whether imposing
the restriction moves the fitted inflation equation. It is not a marginal
likelihood, and it must not be read as a Bayes factor. The formal nested
comparison is an open item in Section~\ref{sec:open}.

Read that way, the answer is clean: the restriction is nearly free. Conditional
DIC moves from \TheoryUConditionalDIC{} in U to \TheoryROneConditionalDIC{} in
R1 --- R1 is in fact marginally \emph{better}, because it spends
\TheoryROneFreeCoefficients{} coefficients instead of U's
\TheoryUFreeCoefficients{} for the same residual fit. RMSE is
\TheoryURmse{} against \TheoryROneRmse. F0, with the fewest coefficients and no
moving reference at all, is not worse than either. On the inflation equation
alone, this data cell cannot tell these models apart.

\section{Q/Q versus four-quarter YoY observation design}
The two inflation transformations are alternative observations of one price
index, and they are estimated in separate likelihoods --- never stacked as if
they were independent information.

The restricted models are Q/Q-only in this revision, and this is a
identification decision rather than a convenience. The cross-equation identity
is a statement about a \emph{quarterly} structural coefficient. A direct
four-quarter regression estimates a reduced-form coefficient whose relation to
the quarterly one depends on the dynamics being aggregated, so imposing
$100\kappa_N=b_x\zeta_0\theta_0$ on it would attach the structural meaning to
the wrong object. The code enforces this: a restricted model with
\code{inflation\_observation=yoy\_4q} raises rather than runs. F0 and U carry the
observation-design comparison instead, because neither imposes the identity.

\par\medskip
\input{../results/tables/theory/inflation_observation_comparison.tex}

\par\medskip\noindent
The comparison is reassuring for the level of the slope and unhelpful for its
response: $\kappa_0$ and $\theta_0$ are similar across the two designs, while
$\kappa_N$ changes sign between them in U. That is the same non-identification
seen in Section~5, viewed from a second angle.

\section{Scope condition and the limits of identification}
Three limits bound what this report can claim, and each is measured rather than
asserted.

\textbf{The local approximation spans a wide $N$ range.} The reference in
Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition} moves several ten-log-point units over the
sample. A local linearisation is being asked to hold across that whole span,
and the admissibility diagnostics are the instrument for detecting strain.

\textbf{Admissibility pressure is high.} In U, \TheoryUProbePercent\% of
unconstrained Gaussian probe draws would violate the imposed region, and
\TheoryUStateRejectPercent\% of proposed latent paths are rejected by whole-path
positivity; R1 is comparable. The probe share does not enter the posterior --- it
is a measurement of how much the constraints are doing --- but a share this high
is exactly the signal the diagnostic was built to raise, and every moving-reference
model is flagged for review on it.

\textbf{$\gamma$ and $\zeta_0$ are not identified by this cell.} $\gamma$'s
posterior tracks its prior, and the implied elasticity has
$P(\zeta_0>1)=\TheoryUProbZetaAboveOne$. Claims that rest on either --- the weak
Third law, or a markup read off the estimated elasticity --- are not supported by
this data cell, whatever the restricted models' point estimates look like.

\section{Robustness}
Two robustness dimensions are settled in this revision and two are not.

\textbf{Settled: observation design.} Section~7 estimates the same cell under
both inflation transformations for the two models entitled to it.

\textbf{Settled: restriction depth.} The hierarchy is itself a robustness
exercise. R1 against U isolates the cross-equation identity; R2 against R1
isolates the weak Third law; R3 against R1 isolates constant pass-through. All
four models are estimated on identical data, priors, and sample, and the builder
refuses to tabulate them together if any of those differ.

\textbf{Not settled: prior sensitivity.} The historical report varies the prior
set (\code{tight}, \code{weak}) across every cell. The restriction namespace has
only \code{\TheoryPriorSpec} runs. Given that $\gamma$'s posterior is
prior-dominated and R1's $\kappa_N$ prior is induced rather than stated, a
prior sweep is the single most valuable missing robustness check here, not an
optional extra.

\textbf{Not settled: sampled $\zeta_0$.} $\zeta_0$ is fixed at
\TheoryZetaZero{} throughout. Section~5 shows the data cannot pin it, so a
sampled-$\zeta_0$ specification would need either a different data cell or an
informative external prior.

\section{Conclusion}
On this data cell, the local Rotemberg--HSA cross-equation restriction is
neither rejected nor confirmed --- and the reason is identification, not
tension. The unrestricted model's posterior for $\kappa_N$ is centred near zero
with a wide two-sided interval that comfortably contains the restricted value,
so the restriction survives; but the same width means the data would have
tolerated almost any value, so surviving is weak evidence.

What the restriction does supply is identification. It converts a
near-uninformative $\kappa_N$ into one that is positive and bounded away from
zero, at essentially no cost in the fit of the inflation equation and with one
fewer free coefficient. The Second law is maintained rather than tested by that
route; the weak Third law is maintained rather than tested outright, since
$\gamma$ is prior-dominated.

The fixed-reference benchmark F0 is the quiet result. It fits as well as
anything else here with the fewest coefficients and no moving reference at all.
Any claim that the moving-reference machinery earns its keep needs the marginal
likelihood that this revision does not yet compute.

\section{Status and open items}\label{sec:open}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Marginal likelihood for the theory namespace.} Chib's method is
    implemented for the historical models only. Without it there is no formal
    test of the nested restrictions, and Section~6 must be read as a
    conditional in-sample diagnostic. This is the largest gap.
  \item \textbf{Prior robustness sweep.} \code{tight} and \code{weak} runs for
    all five models; required to separate the restriction's effect from its
    induced prior on $\kappa_N$.
  \item \textbf{U is not literally unrestricted.} With path positivity on by
    default, U maintains $\kt>0$ and $\theta_t>0$ at every date, so its model
    definition records \code{second\_law: false} while the domain condition is
    doing part of the same work. The $U\to R1$ comparison isolates the
    cross-equation identity, not the Second law.
  \item \textbf{A four-quarter aggregation likelihood.} Until four quarterly
    structural equations can be aggregated without changing coefficient meaning,
    R1--R3 remain Q/Q-only.
  \item \textbf{Admissibility pressure.} Every moving-reference model is flagged
    for local-range review. Either the sample must be split into ranges over
    which the linearisation is defensible, or the approximation must be replaced.
  \item \textbf{R4.} The fixed-reference identities $\kappa_0=(\zeta_0-1)/\chi$
    and $\theta_0=(10/\chi)(1-\rho_0)/\rho_0$ remain deliberately un-imposed.
\end{enumerate}
